<h1>What Is AI? A Technology We Meet Every Day</h1>
<blockquote>For people meeting AI for the first time, let’s begin with the kinds of AI we already use in everyday life.<br /></blockquote>

<h2>Let’s start with what AI means.</h2>
<p>AI is short for <strong>artificial intelligence</strong>.<br />It refers to technologies designed to help computers carry out tasks that usually require human intelligence.<br /></p>
<p>Those tasks can include understanding language, recognizing images, making judgments from information, and making predictions.<br />Google Cloud describes AI as a set of technologies that enables computers to learn, reason, and perform advanced tasks such as understanding language, analyzing data, and making helpful suggestions.<br /><a href="https://cloud.google.com/learn/what-is-artificial-intelligence">Read Google Cloud’s official introduction to AI</a><br /></p>
<p>That does not mean AI has a human mind or feelings.<br />It is more useful to think of AI as a system that finds patterns in information and performs a given task, rather than as something that feels and thinks like a person.<br /></p>
<figure><img src="{{ '/assets/images/easy-ai-01-helping-result.png' | relative_url }}" alt="An AI assistant handing a completed result to a person"></figure>

<h2>AI is already around us.</h2>
<p>When people hear “AI,” they may first think of ChatGPT or image-generation tools.<br />But AI had already been part of many everyday services long before those tools became familiar.<br /></p>
<ul>
<li>When a video service recommends something you might like</li>
<li>When a navigation app suggests a route that avoids traffic</li>
<li>When a phone finds faces or text in a photo</li>
<li>When an email service filters spam</li>
<li>When a keyboard predicts the next word you may type</li>
</ul>
<p>What these examples share is that a computer uses information to make a <strong>judgment, classification, or prediction</strong>.<br /></p>
<figure><img src="{{ '/assets/images/easy-ai-01-walking-recommendation.png' | relative_url }}" alt="AI recommending content that fits a person’s situation"></figure>

<h2>How does AI make a judgment?</h2>
<p>Consider spam email.<br />An AI system does not “dislike” a message.<br />Instead, it can examine words, senders, and writing patterns that often appeared in messages previously labeled as spam, then calculate how likely a new message is to be spam.<br /></p>
<p>That can be useful, but it does not guarantee the right answer every time.<br />If the information used for training is limited or a new situation appears, the result can be different or wrong.<br /></p>
<figure><img src="{{ '/assets/images/easy-ai-01-simple-patterns.png' | relative_url }}" alt="AI finding common patterns across several examples"></figure>

<h2>Think of AI as a tool.</h2>
<p>AI is less like magic that completely replaces people and more like a tool that can help people find, organize, and try things more quickly.<br /></p>
<p>People still need to set the goal, check the result, and make important decisions.<br /></p>

<h2>In one sentence.</h2>
<p class="summary"><strong>AI is technology that helps computers perform tasks that need human intelligence, and it is already part of everyday life through recommendations, search, and autocomplete.</strong><br /></p>
<p>In the next course, we will look at the difference between <strong>machine learning</strong>, a way for AI to learn from data, and <strong>generative AI</strong>, which creates outputs such as text, images, and code.<br /></p>
