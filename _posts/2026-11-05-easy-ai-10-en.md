<h1>Who Owns the Copyright<br />in an AI-Generated Result?</h1>

<blockquote>When AI creates text, images, or music, it can feel as if the result is automatically yours to use.<br />But using AI does not automatically create copyright, and it does not remove other people’s rights.<br />This lesson separates human creative contribution from the rights attached to the material you use.<br /></blockquote>

<figure><img src="{{ '/assets/images/easy-ai-10-copyright-natural.svg' | relative_url }}" alt="An artist selects one of several AI-generated landscapes on a tablet and edits the chosen scene by hand on paper oriented toward them."></figure>

<h2>Copyright protects creative works. <a class="source-badge" href="https://www.copyright.gov/ai/">U.S. Copyright Office guidance</a></h2>

<p><strong>Copyright</strong> is a set of rights that can protect creative works such as writing, images, and music.<br />It can give a creator control over ways others copy, distribute, or publicly use a work, but the scope and exceptions depend on the country and the type of work.<br /></p>

<p>The fact that AI produced a result does not automatically give a person copyright in the whole result.<br />The U.S. Copyright Office explains that copyright analysis should distinguish the parts where a person contributed creative expression from the parts generated autonomously by an AI system.<br />This lesson uses the U.S. framework as an introduction, not as legal advice or a rule for every country.<br /></p>

<h2>A short prompt and a person’s own creation may be treated differently.</h2>

<p>If you enter a short prompt such as “draw a seaside town” and receive an image, you supplied an idea and instructions, but you may not have directly chosen the final expressive details.<br />Whether the whole result qualifies as a human-authored work is not decided automatically.<br /></p>

<p>If you create a draft, select parts of an AI result, change the composition, rewrite sentences, and combine elements through creative choices, the human contribution may be clearer.<br />But simply saying “I edited it” is not enough by itself; the relevant question is what creative decisions the person actually made.<br /></p>

<h2>Check the rights in the material you provide to AI.</h2>

<p>Putting your own photo, writing, or music into an AI tool is different from uploading someone else’s material without permission.<br />If you use another person’s image, character, voice, or document, check permission, licensing terms, and attribution requirements first.<br /></p>

<p>Even when a service says that its output may be used commercially, that does not automatically resolve third-party copyright, trademark, likeness, or voice rights in the result.<br />Before publishing, check whether the result is unusually similar to a known work or person, keep source records, and have a person review it.<br /></p>

<h2>Record these four things before you publish.</h2>

<p><strong>First, what did you create yourself?</strong><br />Keep the draft, photo, composition, wording, and editing decisions that show your creative contribution.<br /></p>

<p><strong>Second, what did you provide to the AI?</strong><br />Record the sources and usage terms for the text, images, or audio you supplied, and verify permission or licensing for other people’s material.<br /></p>

<p><strong>Third, which service and settings did you use?</strong><br />Save the service name, date, important settings, generation steps, and later edits.<br /></p>

<p><strong>Fourth, what did a person review before publication?</strong><br />Check similarity, rights concerns, factual errors, personal information, and commercial-use conditions before sharing the result.<br /></p>

<blockquote>A label saying “AI-generated” does not settle every rights question.<br />Start by recording the human creative contribution and the sources of the material you used.<br /></blockquote>

<h2>One-line takeaway.</h2>

<p><strong>Do not assume an AI result is automatically yours; check the human creative contribution, source rights, attribution, and publication conditions, then keep a record.</strong><br /></p>
