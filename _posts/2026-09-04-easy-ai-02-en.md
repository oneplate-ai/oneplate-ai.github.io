<h1>Machine Learning and Generative AI: What Is the Difference?</h1>
<blockquote>There are several ways to prepare AI inside the same big plate.<br />This time, we will separate how AI learns from data from how it creates new results.<br /></blockquote>

<h2>What does machine learning learn?</h2>
<p><strong>Machine learning</strong> is a way of training computers to examine a large amount of <strong>data</strong>, find rules or patterns in it, and produce a result.<br /></p>
<p>Google for Developers describes machine learning as a way to train software, called a model, to make predictions or generate content from data.<br /><a href="https://developers.google.com/machine-learning/intro-to-ml/what-is-ml">Read Google’s official explanation of machine learning</a><br /></p>

<h2>Machine learning that makes predictions.</h2>
<p>For example, if a system is shown a great deal of past weather data, it may find patterns between information such as temperature, humidity, and clouds and the amount of rain that followed.<br />When it receives new weather information, it can then <strong>predict</strong> how much rain may fall.<br />Spam filters, estimated arrival times in navigation apps, and video recommendations can also involve prediction or classification.<br /></p>
<p>A machine-learning result is a prediction based on patterns found in past data, not a judgment that guarantees the correct answer.<br /></p>

<h2>What does generative AI create?</h2>
<p><strong>Generative AI</strong> is AI that creates <strong>new outputs</strong>, such as text, images, music, or code, from patterns it has learned.<br /></p>
<p>It can help to think of AI tasks in two broad groups: <strong>prediction and classification</strong>, and <strong>content generation</strong>.<br />Looking at a photo and deciding whether it shows a cat, or estimating the chance of rain tomorrow, is closer to prediction or classification.<br />Creating something new from an instruction is closer to generative AI.<br /></p>
<p>If you ask, “What should I pack for camping on a rainy day?”, generative AI can compose an answer from the meaning of the question and the language patterns it learned.<br />When asked for an image, it combines shapes and colors; when asked for a song, it combines musical patterns; and when asked for code, it can draft the commands that may be needed.<br />The important point is that generative AI does not have a finished answer stored in its mind or experience the world as a person does.<br />It produces an output step by step by calculating what fits the input and its learned patterns.<br /></p>
<p>For that reason, generative AI is better understood as producing a new output by combining learned patterns than as simply copying its training material.<br />That still does not guarantee that every result is factual or original.<br /></p>
<p>Google’s machine-learning materials similarly describe generative AI as learning patterns from existing data to create new content such as text, images, and music.<br /><a href="https://developers.google.com/machine-learning/intro-to-ml/what-is-ml">Read the same official explanation of generative AI</a><br /></p>

<h2>What kinds of generative AI are there?</h2>
<p>Generative AI is not the name of one service. It is a term for technology that creates new outputs.<br />The services people use can look different depending on the kind of output they make.<br /></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Text and conversation</strong>: services such as ChatGPT, Gemini, and Claude that answer questions and help organize writing</li>
<li><strong>Images</strong>: services such as Midjourney and Adobe Firefly that create images from a description</li>
<li><strong>Code</strong>: services such as GitHub Copilot that suggest and explain programming code</li>
</ul>
<p>These services are made by different companies and have different features and access conditions.<br />A single service may also work with text, images, audio, and code, so these categories are simply a way to make the idea easier to understand.<br /></p>
<figure><img src="{{ '/assets/images/easy-ai-02-generative-types.png' | relative_url }}" alt="Examples of generative AI for text, images, and code"></figure>

<h2>Are they completely different technologies?</h2>
<p>Not exactly.<br /><strong>Generative AI is also a system built with machine learning in the broad sense.</strong><br />What differs is how we look at the result.<br /></p>
<ul>
<li>Machine learning: classifies information or predicts a number from the information it is given.</li>
<li>Generative AI: creates a new output from the patterns it has learned.</li>
</ul>
<p>Estimating whether it will rain tomorrow is closer to prediction; writing a story about a rainy day is closer to generation.<br /></p>

<h2>Generative-AI answers still need checking.</h2>
<p>Creating new content does not mean an AI system always creates an accurate result.<br />It can produce a plausible but incorrect answer when information is limited or when it misunderstands the question.<br />When you use generative AI for something important, check the sources and review names, dates, and numbers again.<br /></p>

<h2>In one sentence.</h2>
<p class="summary"><strong>Machine learning uses data to classify and predict, while generative AI uses learned patterns to create new text, images, music, code, and other outputs.</strong><br /></p>
