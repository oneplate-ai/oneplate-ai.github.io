<h1>September 7, 2026</h1>
<blockquote>Today’s news looks at AI making a mathematical proof machine-checkable, helping researchers with experiments, and raising new questions about safety standards as capabilities advance faster.<br /></blockquote>

<h2>1. Claude formalizes Fermat’s Last Theorem for computer checking <a class="source-badge" href="https://www.anthropic.com/news/formalizing-fermats-last-theorem">Anthropic announcement</a></h2>
<p class="multi-sentence">Anthropic announced that Claude worked largely autonomously for 11 days with Lean, a language for checking mathematical proofs, to formalize a complete proof of Fermat’s Last Theorem.<br />The theorem states that, for any integer n greater than 2, there are no positive integers a, b, and c that satisfy aⁿ + bⁿ = cⁿ.<br /></p>
<p class="multi-sentence">According to Anthropic, Claude wrote about 13 million lines of Lean code and proved 29,500 intermediate theorems for the final proof.<br />The completed proof was checked with Lean, and the full materials were released on <a href="https://github.com/anthropics/fermats-last-theorem">GitHub</a>.<br /></p>
<ul><li><strong>Who it may help:</strong> Mathematics researchers and people developing or learning proof-verification tools</li><li><strong>Availability:</strong> The proof code and explanation are public.<br />That does not mean a general user can immediately reproduce a proof at the same scale with Claude.</li><li><strong>Why it matters:</strong> Instead of requiring people to read every step of an AI-produced mathematical result from the beginning, proof assistants can help check the logical chain.</li><li><strong>What to watch:</strong> The achievement and its meaning are described by Anthropic.<br />Computer checking verifies the logic of a formalized proof, but it does not replace choosing a problem or writing an explanation people can understand.</li></ul>

<h2>2. OpenAI says it has reached an automated research intern milestone <a class="source-badge" href="https://openai.com/index/research-acceleration-view-inside-openai/">OpenAI announcement</a></h2>
<p class="multi-sentence">OpenAI defines an “automated research intern” as a system that can carry out well-defined research tasks under human direction, including tasks that could take a skilled researcher a few days.<br />The company says it reached its September goal this year, and that researchers are increasingly using coding agents across multiple sessions to write code and run experiments.<br /></p>
<p class="multi-sentence">OpenAI says people still set research priorities, judge which ideas and results to pursue, and decide whether to scale, pause, or deploy systems.<br />It also says it is making progress toward an automated AI researcher by March 2028.<br /></p>
<ul><li><strong>Who it may help:</strong> AI research and development teams, and organizations automating repeated coding and experimental work</li><li><strong>Availability:</strong> This post describes OpenAI’s internal research use.<br />It is not an announcement of a separate product for general users.</li><li><strong>Why it matters:</strong> AI may become part of the research process by writing code and repeating experiments, rather than only generating answers.</li><li><strong>What to watch:</strong> The usage growth and research-acceleration effects are OpenAI’s account based on internal measurements and observations.<br />They are not independent evidence that the entire research process is accelerating at the same rate.</li></ul>

<h2>3. OpenAI’s Chief Scientist calls for shared safety standards as AI advances <a class="source-badge" href="https://openai.com/index/an-alien-mind/">OpenAI announcement</a></h2>
<p class="multi-sentence">OpenAI Chief Scientist Jakub Pachocki wrote that reasoning language models are advancing toward operating computers and graphical interfaces, working with people and other AI systems, and carrying out research projects.<br />At the same time, he argued that these systems can generalize differently from people, making their behavior in situations outside their training difficult to predict with confidence.<br /></p>
<p class="multi-sentence">In this context, <strong>alignment</strong> means making AI act in ways that fit human goals and values.<br />Pachocki wrote that no lab has solved alignment and monitoring well enough to keep scaling at maximum speed responsibly for much longer, and called for shared safety standards and international coordination.<br /></p>
<ul><li><strong>Who it may help:</strong> Organizations adopting AI, policymakers, and the general public</li><li><strong>Availability:</strong> This is not a product announcement.<br />It is OpenAI’s Chief Scientist’s view and a discussion of safety questions.</li><li><strong>Why it matters:</strong> When looking at AI capability competition, it is important to consider monitorability, human control, and responsibility as well as features and speed.</li><li><strong>What to watch:</strong> Forecasts about what level AI may reach in the next few years are predictions, not settled facts.<br />The claims in this essay draw on OpenAI’s internal results and the author’s judgment, and should be distinguished from independent consensus or verification.</li></ul>

<h2>Today in one sentence</h2>
<p class="summary">Today’s three stories show AI moving further into the process of producing and checking research results, while raising the question of how people can supervise that progress.<br />When you meet a new capability claim or forecast, it helps to separate what was actually checked, who checked it, and what remains an opinion or prediction.<br /></p>
