<p class="post-kicker">Daily AI Bite</p>
<h1>September 25, 2026</h1>
<blockquote>This issue looks at how AI connects to other apps, video-based enterprise AI agents, and research that tests AI hardware in space.<br />Feature scope, real access conditions, and experimental results all need to be checked separately from an announcement.<br /></blockquote>

<h2>1. Gemini is expanding its Connected Apps. <a class="source-badge" href="https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/products/gemini-app/new-connected-apps-gemini/">Google announcement</a></h2>
<p class="multi-sentence">On September 23, Google announced a gradual rollout of new Connected Apps for Gemini.<br />It says Gemini can connect with productivity tools such as Airtable, Linear, monday.com, PandaDoc, and Zoho, as well as creative tools such as Adobe and Webflow.<br /></p>
<p class="multi-sentence">Connected Apps do not mean that AI handles every task by itself.<br />They are a way to bring information from tools a person chooses into a conversation or continue work across those tools, so checking what data and permissions are shared remains important.<br /></p>
<ul><li><strong>Who it may help:</strong> Gemini users organizing project information or work across multiple productivity and creative tools.</li><li><strong>Current status:</strong> Google said the new apps would begin rolling out on September 23. The apps available to connect, along with regional and account access, may vary.<br /></li><li><strong>Why it matters:</strong> It shows AI services moving beyond a standalone answer box toward connections with the tools people already use.<br /></li><li><strong>What to watch:</strong> Before connecting an app, check the permission scope and shared data, and review any important change or transfer yourself.<br /></li></ul>

<h2>2. Gemini 3.8 Live added a video-avatar feature. <a class="source-badge" href="https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/gemini-3-8-live-with-live-avatar/">Google announcement</a></h2>
<p class="multi-sentence">On September 24, Google introduced Gemini 3.8 Live with Live Avatar.<br />The feature combines near-real-time video generation and speech to create conversations with a visual avatar that can listen, see, and speak.<br /></p>
<p class="multi-sentence">Google presented it for enterprise conversations such as customer support and guided experiences, including background tool calls that can continue while a conversation remains active.<br />Google also says its generated audio and video carry a SynthID watermark, but that does not remove every risk of confusion or misuse.<br /></p>
<ul><li><strong>Who it may help:</strong> Teams building enterprise conversational services that need both voice and visual interaction, such as customer guidance, training, or support.</li><li><strong>Current status:</strong> Google says the feature is available in Gemini Enterprise. It says custom avatar creation is available only through enterprise allowlisting.<br /></li><li><strong>Why it matters:</strong> Conversational AI interfaces are expanding from text and voice into video-based expression.<br /></li><li><strong>What to watch:</strong> AI that appears human should be clearly disclosed as AI, with rules for privacy, identity representation, and incorrect answers designed alongside it.<br /></li></ul>

<h2>3. Google outlined Project Suncatcher, a space test for AI hardware. <a class="source-badge" href="https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/google-research/google-project-suncatcher-facts/">Google announcement</a></h2>
<p class="multi-sentence">On September 24, Google described an early orbital-test plan for Project Suncatcher.<br />The long-term research project will test whether TPUs carried on a satellite can withstand vibration, radiation, and vacuum conditions while running AI workloads.<br /></p>
<p class="multi-sentence">Google described abundant sunlight in low Earth orbit and laser links between satellites as long-term goals.<br />This stage is not an announcement of an operational space AI data center; it is an early test intended to measure hardware survival, cooling, and communications challenges.<br /></p>
<ul><li><strong>Who it may help:</strong> Readers and developers interested in long-term research on AI infrastructure, semiconductors, and space communications.</li><li><strong>Current status:</strong> Google presents the first TPU orbital test as an initial experiment and names 2027 as a target for a later inter-satellite connectivity test.<br /></li><li><strong>Why it matters:</strong> It is a research direction that considers AI compute’s power, cooling, and communications constraints beyond ground-based data centers.<br /></li><li><strong>What to watch:</strong> Power, launch, radiation, cooling, and communication remain open engineering challenges, so it is too early to treat this as commercial AI infrastructure.<br /></li></ul>

<h2>Today in one sentence.</h2>
<p class="summary">AI is reaching more apps, screens, and infrastructure, but people still need to check connection permissions, user disclosure, and the experimental stage of each claim.<br /></p>
