<p class="post-kicker">Weekly AI Platter</p>
<h1>September 12–September 18, 2026</h1>
<p class="summary"><strong>This week’s one actually published AI news post shows that as AI moves deeper into work and creative production, execution evidence and human review need to move with it.</strong><br /></p>

<h2>1. AI coding is moving toward showing execution evidence, not only claiming that a result was made. <a class="source-badge" href="https://openai.com/index/cognition-devin-testing-with-astra">OpenAI announcement</a></h2>
<p class="multi-sentence">Monday’s September 14 post covered a case in which Cognition’s Devin used GPT-6 Astra to test software it had created and returned a recording of an iPhone game running in a simulator alongside a report of passed checks.<br />The approach tries to show what the AI actually ran and how much of the result was checked, rather than stopping at the statement that AI wrote the code.<br /></p>
<p class="multi-sentence">The case study and capability descriptions come from OpenAI and Cognition, so development teams still need to judge whether the recording and report cover the risks that matter for their software.<br /></p>

<h2>2. AI can become an interactive creative element in extended-reality storytelling. <a class="source-badge" href="https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/xr-ar/three-google-supported-projects-premiere-during-the-83rd-venice-international-film-festival/">Google announcement</a></h2>
<p class="multi-sentence">The same post covered Google’s three extended-reality (XR) projects presented at the 83rd Venice International Film Festival.<br />XR overlays digital visuals or interaction onto a real-world setting, and the projects use Gemini and Vertex AI.<br /></p>
<p class="multi-sentence">The Gemini-powered mixed-reality animation “NEVATARS” and an immersive nature experience with a conversational digital Charles Darwin show AI becoming part of a story’s interaction rather than only generating text or images.<br />Actual access and experience quality can vary by project, device, and venue.<br /></p>

<h2>One proposal argues that AI capability growth and safety checks should move at a pace that keeps them aligned. <a class="source-badge" href="https://darioamodei.com/post/we-must-pace-the-frontier">Dario Amodei’s essay</a></h2>
<p class="multi-sentence">Monday’s September 14 post covered Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei’s proposal to slow the rate at which AI models gain capabilities so that safety research and risk prevention can keep up.<br />He described this “pacing” not as stopping development, but as making sure safety, alignment, and security checks happen before models become more capable.<br /></p>
<p class="multi-sentence">The CEOs of OpenAI and xAI publicly responded to the direction, but this is not an industry-wide agreement or legal obligation.<br />Implementation should be judged through verifiable follow-up actions, such as the access given to independent evaluators and how incidents are disclosed.<br /></p>

<h2>This week’s direction.</h2>
<p class="multi-sentence">Only the September 14 Daily AI Bite post was actually published among this week’s Monday, Wednesday, and Friday slots, but it still shows AI expansion and the need for verification growing together.<br /></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Execution:</strong> AI-produced results should be assessed with checkable evidence such as recordings, tests, and reports.</li>
<li><strong>Creativity:</strong> AI can become an interactive part of new story formats, while access and experience conditions differ by project.</li>
<li><strong>Safety:</strong> Capability growth needs to be managed alongside safety research and outside evaluation.</li>
</ul>
<p class="multi-sentence">When you meet a new AI announcement, ask not only “What can it do?” but also “What evidence shows it ran?”, “Who checks the result?”, and “How is safety being evaluated?”<br /></p>
