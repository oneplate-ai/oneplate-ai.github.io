<h1>The Context Window<br />Is AI’s Workspace.</h1>

<blockquote>AI can use earlier messages and documents, but the information it can consider at one time has limits.<br />This lesson explains context windows and how to handle a long conversation more reliably.<br /></blockquote>

<figure><img src="{{ '/assets/images/easy-ai-08-context-window.svg' | relative_url }}" alt="A person organizes notes in one workspace while an AI refers to the material"></figure>

<h2>A context window is the workspace an AI can use at one time. <a class="source-badge" href="https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/long-context">Google documentation</a></h2>

<p><strong>Context window</strong> means the range of input and conversation that an AI can use when creating one response.<br />It is useful to think of it as short-term memory or a workspace that holds the material needed for the current answer.<br /></p>

<p>The material is not limited to the latest question.<br />Depending on the service and model, it can include earlier messages, pasted documents, images, and other input.<br /></p>

<h2>Tokens are the units used to measure that space.</h2>

<p><strong>Tokens</strong> are small units that an AI uses to process text and other inputs.<br />A word is not always one token; a word can be split into several tokens depending on the language and expression.<br /></p>

<p>That is why context windows are often described in tokens rather than characters.<br />As your question and reference material grow, they use more of the workspace, while the response also needs room to be generated.<br /></p>

<h2>Older parts of a long conversation may be removed. <a class="source-badge" href="https://platform.openai.com/docs/api-reference/realtime-client-events/session">OpenAI documentation</a></h2>

<p>When a conversation and its material become larger than the context window, the full history cannot keep being included.<br />A service may shorten, summarize, or leave out older messages before generating the next response.<br /></p>

<p>That is why a detail from the beginning of a conversation can become less effective later.<br />If an AI seems to forget an earlier agreement, the reason may be that the information is no longer included in the current input, not that the system has human-like memory.<br /></p>

<h2>A large document does not mean every part will be used equally well.</h2>

<p>A large context window is useful when you need to work with a long document or several files at once.<br />But as the amount of material grows, it can become harder to find the important part, separate conflicting details, and connect the question to the right passage.<br /></p>

<p>So “the model can accept the material” is not the same as “the model will use every part accurately.”<br />Google’s long-context guidance recommends providing only what is needed and placing a clear question after the reference material when appropriate.<br /></p>

<h2>Keep the workspace organized for a long task.</h2>

<p><strong>First, choose only the material you need.</strong><br />Removing unrelated messages and documents makes it easier to focus on the current question.<br /></p>

<p><strong>Second, repeat the important constraints.</strong><br />Briefly restate the goal, audience, deadline, and prohibitions near the end of a long request when they must not be missed.<br /></p>

<p><strong>Third, leave a checkpoint summary.</strong><br />Have a person confirm the decisions and remaining work, then use that summary as the starting point for a new conversation when needed.<br /></p>

<p><strong>Fourth, compare important results with the original.</strong><br />Even a large context window can still lead to a misunderstanding or omission, so check important numbers, conditions, and quotations against the source.<br /></p>

<h2>One-line takeaway.</h2>

<p><strong>A context window is the workspace an AI can use for a response, so long tasks work better when you restate the needed material and key constraints.</strong><br /></p>
